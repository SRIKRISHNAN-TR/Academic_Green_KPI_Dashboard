================================================================================
                              ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The Academic Green KPI Dashboard is a full-stack web application designed to
address the growing need for data-driven environmental sustainability management
in academic institutions. Academic campuses consist of numerous facilities such
as academic blocks, laboratories, libraries, hostels, and administrative offices,
each consuming significant amounts of energy, water, and generating waste.
Without a centralized system, monitoring and managing resource consumption
across all these locations is a complex and inefficient task.
This project presents a comprehensive solution that enables institutions to
track, analyze, and report on four key environmental metrics: Electricity
Consumption (kWh), Water Usage (m³), Waste Diversion Rate (%), and Carbon
Footprint (tCO2e). The system is built on a modern MERN stack comprising
MongoDB, Express.js, React 18, and Node.js, following a clean RESTful API
architecture.
The application features an intelligent RAG (Red-Amber-Green) status system
that automatically evaluates Month-to-Date (MTD) and Year-to-Date (YTD)
performance against predefined sustainability targets. It provides role-based
access control (Admin, Data Entry, Public User) to ensure data integrity and
security. Advanced analytics features include highest-usage location ranking,
an overall Sustainability Score, and trend visualization through interactive
charts. The system also supports KPI Snapshot generation for historical tracking
and report export in PDF and DOCX formats. Notifications and email alerts are
triggered automatically when thresholds are breached.
The project is deployed using a cloud-based architecture with the frontend
hosted on Vercel and the backend connected to MongoDB Atlas for high availability.
This solution serves as a centralized, intelligent platform to help academic
institutions reduce their carbon footprint, meet sustainability goals, and
support initiatives like NAAC greening criteria and NIRF India Rankings.
Keywords: KPI Dashboard, Sustainability, MERN Stack, React, MongoDB, REST API,
          Environmental Monitoring, Role-Based Access Control.

1.1 OVERVIEW
------------
The escalating global concern over environmental sustainability has led many
educational institutions to adopt green initiatives as part of their core
administrative functions. Academic campuses, by virtue of their size and
operational scale, consume enormous quantities of electricity, water, and
generate substantial amounts of waste on a daily basis.

The "Academic Green KPI Dashboard" is a web-based platform specifically
engineered for academic institutions to monitor, track, and manage their
environmental Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) in real time. It replaces
traditional, manual, and error-prone spreadsheet-based tracking methods with
a centralized digital system that is accessible to administrators, data entry
personnel, and the general public (for transparency).

The system consolidates data from multiple campus locations and presents
performance metrics in a visual, intuitive format, enabling decision-makers to
identify underperforming areas and take corrective action swiftly.


1.2 PROBLEM STATEMENT
---------------------
Academic institutions face several challenges in managing their environmental
footprint:

(i)   Lack of Centralization: Environmental data (energy bills, water meter
      readings, waste logs) is maintained in isolated spreadsheets across
      departments, making institution-wide analysis difficult.

(ii)  No Real-Time Visibility: Data is often reviewed monthly or quarterly,
      meaning problems are identified too late for timely intervention.

(iii) Absence of Target Tracking: Without structured targets, there is no
      mechanism to measure whether the institution is improving or regressing.

(iv)  Poor Reporting: Generating formatted reports for accreditation bodies
      (NAAC, NIRF) requires significant manual effort.

(v)   Limited Accountability: Without role-based access, data accuracy and
      responsibility are hard to enforce.

This project addresses all of the above challenges through a purpose-built
web application.


1.3 OBJECTIVES
--------------
The primary objectives of this project are:

1. To develop a centralized web-based platform for tracking environmental KPIs
   across an academic campus.

2. To implement an automated Green/Yellow/Red (RAG) status system that
   evaluates actual performance against sustainability targets.

3. To provide Month-to-Date (MTD) and Year-to-Date (YTD) performance summaries
   for Energy, Water, Waste, and Carbon metrics.

4. To enable role-based access for Admin, Data Entry, and Public User roles.

5. To deliver interactive dashboards with charts, trend analysis, and location-
   wise rankings.

6. To support KPI Snapshot generation and export of reports to PDF and DOCX.

7. To send automated notifications and email alerts when thresholds are breached.

8. To deploy the solution on a scalable cloud infrastructure.


1.4 SCOPE OF THE PROJECT
------------------------
The scope of the Academic Green KPI Dashboard covers the following areas:

In-Scope:
  - Tracking four environmental metrics: Electricity, Water, Waste, and Carbon.
  - Multi-location support within a single institution.
  - User authentication, authorization, and role management.
  - Data entry, target setting, and performance evaluation.
  - Dashboard visualization, trend analysis, and summary reporting.
  - Export functionality (PDF, DOCX).

Out-of-Scope:

2.1 EXISTING SYSTEMS & LIMITATIONS
-----------------------------------
Several sustainability tracking platforms exist, but each has notable
limitations when applied to academic institutions:

1. IBM Environmental Intelligence Suite: A powerful enterprise tool, but
   is cost-prohibitive and complex for a single academic institution.

2. Microsoft Sustainability Manager: Requires extensive Azure cloud
   infrastructure and is primarily aimed at large corporations.

3. SpreadSheet-Based Tracking (Manual): Widely used in Indian colleges.
   Limitations include: no real-time access, prone to human error, no
   automated status evaluation, and poor scalability.

4. Verdafero / Resource Advisor (Oracle): Advanced IoT-integrated platforms
   that require specialized hardware sensors, making them impractical for
   most academic institutions in India.

None of these solutions offer a low-cost, easy-to-deploy, web-based platform
specifically tailored to the organizational structure and reporting requirements
of academic institutions in India (NAAC, NIRF criteria).


2.2 RELEVANT TECHNOLOGIES & FRAMEWORKS
---------------------------------------

A) MongoDB (NoSQL Database):
   MongoDB is a document-oriented NoSQL database that stores data in flexible
   JSON-like documents. It is ideal for this project because of the variability
   in environmental data attributes across different metrics (some have location,
   others do not). Its rich aggregation pipeline enables efficient MTD/YTD
   calculations directly at the database level.

B) Express.js:
   A minimal and flexible Node.js web application framework that provides a
   robust set of features for building RESTful APIs. It was chosen for its
   simplicity, performance, and extensive middleware ecosystem.

C) React 18 with TypeScript:
   React is a declarative JavaScript library for building user interfaces.
   TypeScript adds static typing, reducing runtime errors and improving code
   maintainability, which is critical for a data-heavy dashboard application.

D) Node.js:
   Node.js provides a non-blocking, event-driven runtime environment that
   handles concurrent API requests efficiently, which is important for a
   multi-user dashboard system.

E) Vite:
   A next-generation build tool that provides near-instant Hot Module
   Replacement (HMR) during development and produces optimized production
   bundles significantly faster than traditional tools like Create React App.

F) TanStack React Query:
   A data-fetching and server-state management library for React. It handles
   caching, background refetching, and synchronization with the server,
   reducing the need for complex state management code.

G) JWT (JSON Web Tokens):
   An industry-standard RFC 7519 method for secure information transmission.
   Used here for stateless authentication, avoiding the need for server-side
   session storage.

4.1 SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
------------------------
The application follows a classic 3-Tier Client-Server architecture:

  [Client Browser (React SPA)]
          |
          | HTTPS (REST API calls via Axios)
          |
  [Backend Server (Node.js + Express.js)]
          |
          | Mongoose ODM
          |
  [Database (MongoDB Atlas - Cloud)]

Additionally:
  - JWT tokens are stored on the client (localStorage) and sent with each
    protected API request in the Authorization header.
  - CORS is configured on the server to whitelist the frontend domain only.
  - Rate limiting is applied at the server level on auth endpoints.


4.2 DATABASE SCHEMA DESIGN
---------------------------
The MongoDB database uses separate collections for each entity:

a) users collection (User Model):
   Fields:
   - _id         : ObjectId (auto-generated primary key)
   - username    : String (required, unique)
   - email       : String (required, unique)
   - password    : String (bcrypt-hashed, required)
   - role        : String (enum: "admin" | "data-entry" | "user", default: "user")
   - resetToken  : String (for password reset flow)
   - resetTokenExpiry : Date
   - createdAt   : Date (default: now)

b) energy_data collection (Energy Model):
   Fields:
   - metric   : String (default: "ENERGY")
   - month    : String (e.g., "January")
   - year     : Number
   - actual   : Number (kWh consumed)
   - target   : Number (kWh target)
   - unit     : String (default: "kWh")
   - source   : String (e.g., meter ID)
   - location : String (e.g., "Block A")
   - status   : String (enum: "GREEN" | "YELLOW" | "RED")
   - createdAt: Date
   Index: { year: 1, month: 1, location: 1 }

c) water_data collection (Water Model):
   Fields: same structure as Energy, unit defaults to "m³"
   Index: { year: 1, month: 1, location: 1 }

d) waste_data collection (Waste Model):
   Fields: same structure as Energy, unit defaults to "%"
   Note: Waste uses diversion rate (%), so status logic is inverted —
         higher actual is BETTER (more waste diverted from landfill).
   Index: { year: 1, month: 1, location: 1 }

e) carbon_data collection (Carbon Model):
   Fields: same structure as Energy, unit defaults to "tCO2e"
   Note: Carbon does not have a location field.

f) targets collection (Target Model):
   Fields:
   - metric      : String (enum: "ENERGY" | "WATER" | "WASTE")
   - year        : Number
   - targetValue : Number
   - unit        : String
   - location    : String

g) kpi_snapshots collection (KpiSnapshot Model):
   Fields:
   - metric      : String (enum: "ENERGY" | "WATER" | "WASTE")
   - month       : String
   - year        : Number
   - actual      : Number
   - target      : Number
   - status      : String (enum: "GREEN" | "YELLOW" | "RED")
   - location    : String
   - generatedAt : Date

h) notifications collection (Notification Model):
   Fields:
   - type        : String (enum: "warning" | "info" | "success")
   - title       : String
   - message     : String
   - kpi         : String (e.g., "ENERGY")
   - location    : String
   - actualValue : Number
   - targetValue : Number
   - read        : Boolean (default: false)
   - resolved    : Boolean (default: false)
   - resolvedAt  : Date
   - userId      : ObjectId (ref: User)
   - createdAt   : Date

4.4 MODULE DESIGN
-----------------
The system is divided into the following key modules:

1. Authentication Module:
   - Handles user registration, login, JWT token generation, and password
     reset (via tokenized email links using Nodemailer).

2. Dashboard Module:
   - Aggregates data from Energy, Water, and Waste collections using MongoDB's
     aggregation pipeline.
   - Calculates MTD and YTD summaries, applies RAG logic, computes the
     overall Sustainability Score.
   - Identifies highest-usage locations (top 5 ranked).

3. Data Entry Module:
   - CRUD operations for energy, water, waste, and carbon readings.
   - Client-side form validation using React Hook Form + Zod.
   - Ensures data quality before submission to the backend.

4. Target Management Module:
   - Admins can define annual targets per metric, location, and unit.
   - Targets are retrieved by the Dashboard module for performance evaluation.

5. Trends & Analytics Module:
   - Provides time-series data for interactive line/bar charts (Recharts).
   - Allows filtering by year, metric, and location.

6. Reporting Module:
   - KPI Snapshots freeze the current month's data for historical preservation.
   - Export functionality generates formatted PDF and DOCX reports.

7. Notification Module:
   - In-app alerts stored in the notifications collection.
   - Automated email alerts sent via SMTP/Nodemailer.

8. Admin Module:
   - User Management: View, promote/demote, or delete users.
   - Department/Location Management: Manage campus location entries.


4.5 RAG STATUS CALCULATION LOGIC
---------------------------------
The system uses different logic for Waste (where higher is better) vs.
Energy and Water (where lower is better):

For ENERGY and WATER:
  IF actual <= target                   => GREEN  (on track)
  IF actual <= target * 1.10 (10% over) => YELLOW (warning)
  IF actual > target * 1.10             => RED    (over limit)

For WASTE (diversion rate — higher is better):
  IF actual >= target                   => GREEN  (meeting goal)
  IF actual >= target * 0.90 (10% under)=> YELLOW (near miss)
  IF actual < target * 0.90             => RED    (underperforming)


4.6 SUSTAINABILITY SCORE FORMULA
---------------------------------
The Sustainability Score is computed as an average of per-metric scores:

  For ENERGY/WATER (lower is better):
    score = max(0, (1 - (actual - target) / target)) * 100

  For WASTE (higher is better):
    score = (actual / target) * 100

  Overall Score = (energy_score + water_score + waste_score) / 3

The score ranges from 0 to 100, where 100 indicates perfect adherence
to all sustainability targets.

5.2 BACKEND IMPLEMENTATION
---------------------------
The backend is a RESTful Express.js server organized with a clear separation
of concerns:

Directory Structure:
  backend/
    config/
      db.js              - MongoDB connection via Mongoose
    controllers/
      auth.controller.js
      dashboard.controller.js
      energy.controller.js
      water.controller.js
      waste.controller.js
      target.controller.js
      user.controller.js
      notification.controller.js
    models/
      User.js
      Energy.js

    routes/
      auth.routes.js
      energy.routes.js
      water.routes.js
      waste.routes.js
      target.routes.js
      dashboard.routes.js
      user.routes.js
      notification.routes.js
    server.js            - App entry point

Key Implementation Details:

A) server.js:
   - Configures CORS with a dynamic whitelist from FRONTEND_URL env variable,
     supporting multiple origins (comma-separated).
   - Applies express-rate-limit middleware to /api/auth to prevent brute-force.
   - Connects to MongoDB Atlas and seeds the default admin account on startup.
   - Registers all API route modules.
   - Includes a global 404 handler and a centralized error-handling middleware.

B) dashboard.controller.js (Core Logic):
   - getMetricSummary(): Computes MTD/YTD for a given metric and model.
     Uses MongoDB's aggregation pipeline ($group with $sum/$avg) for
     all-location views, and standard .find() queries for single locations.
     Waste uses averages (it's a rate, not a sum).
   - getSummary(): Runs three parallel getMetricSummary() calls using
     Promise.all() for near-simultaneous fetching. Returns JSON with
     month, year, energy, water, waste objects.
   - getHighestUsage(): Uses an aggregation pipeline to rank top 5 locations
     by total actual usage. Waste ranks by lowest average diversion (worst).
   - generateSnapshot(): Fetches current MTD summary and persists it as
     an immutable KpiSnapshot document.

C) Authentication (auth.controller.js):
   - Registration: Hashes password with bcrypt, saves user with "user" role.
   - Login: Compares bcrypt hash, issues signed JWT (expires in 7 days).
   - Forgot Password: Generates a secure random token, saves expiry (1 hour),
     sends a reset link via Nodemailer SMTP.
   - Reset Password: Validates token and expiry, hashes new password, clears
     the reset token.
   - Seed Admin: On server start, creates a default admin account if none exists.

5.4 AUTHENTICATION & SECURITY
------------------------------
1. Password Security: Bcrypt with 10 salt rounds. Passwords are never stored
   in plaintext or transmitted in any response.

2. JWT Authentication: Tokens are signed with a server-side secret key
   (JWT_SECRET env variable) and expire after 7 days. Protected routes
   validate the token via a middleware function before executing the handler.

3. Role-Based Authorization: Middleware checks user.role against required
   roles. Admin-only endpoints reject requests from Data-Entry or User roles.

4. CORS Whitelisting: The server only accepts requests from the configured
   FRONTEND_URL. Requests from unknown origins receive a CORS policy error.

5. Rate Limiting: Authentication endpoints are capped at 20 requests per
   15-minute window, preventing brute-force login attacks.

6. Password Reset Security: Reset tokens are cryptographically random,
   stored as hashed values, and expire after 1 hour. They are single-use
   (cleared after successful reset).

7.1 DASHBOARD OVERVIEW
-----------------------
The dashboard successfully provides a one-page summary of the institution's
environmental health. The three primary KPI cards display real-time MTD
data with clear RAG status badges. The Usage vs. Target progress bars give
an immediate visual comparison. The Sustainability Score gives the institution
a single number representing overall environmental health.

The Highest Usage Locations panel successfully identifies and ranks the worst-
performing buildings, allowing administrators to direct their conservation
efforts precisely.


7.2 KPI METRIC TRACKING
------------------------
Energy Tracking: Monthly electricity consumption is tracked in kWh per
location. The system correctly aggregates readings from multiple meters
within the same location.

Water Tracking: Monthly water consumption is tracked in m³. The system
identifies high-usage months and locations for targeted interventions.

Waste Diversion: The system correctly handles the inverted logic for waste,
where HIGHER diversion rates improve the institution's RAG status.

Carbon Footprint: Institutions can log their carbon emissions (tCO2e) to
track their carbon neutrality progress over time.

The Trends module provides actionable historical insights. Year-over-year
and month-over-month comparisons help identify seasonal patterns in resource
consumption.

The KPI Snapshot feature ensures that historical performance data is preserved
immutably, which is valuable for longitudinal studies, NAAC green audit reports,
and NIRF India Rankings submissions.
